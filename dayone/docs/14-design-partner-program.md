# 14 · Design partner program

Status: draft v1 · September 2026

## Purpose

Eight to twelve companies that run real candidates through Dayone during the pre-build concierge phase and the MVP, give weekly feedback, and let us collect the data that becomes our validity evidence. They are customers, not friends doing us a favour, and they must convert to paid at the end.

## Who we want

| Profile | Count | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AI-native startups (series A to C) hiring senior and applied engineers | 3 to 4 | Fastest to adopt, best logos for the category, high-visibility engineering leaders |
| Product companies 200 to 2,000 people with an engineering-owned loop | 3 to 4 | The core mid-market target; tests the real buyer |
| One company that currently uses HackerRank or CodeSignal and is unhappy | 1 to 2 | Direct competitive learning; a switching story |
| One company that currently bans AI in interviews and is reconsidering | 1 | Tests the persuasion path for the 34% |
| One recruiting agency placing senior engineers | 1 | Channel learning |

Must-haves: hiring at least 3 engineers in the next 90 days; an engineering leader who will personally join the weekly call; willingness to be quoted if happy.

Avoid: companies with no open roles ("we'd love to try it someday"), companies that want a custom build before running one candidate, and anyone who wants to be a partner in exchange for equity.

## Where to find them

- Founders' and early team's networks (first 4).
- Engineering leaders who have posted publicly about broken interviews or AI in interviews (LinkedIn, X, Blind, Hacker News threads on Interview Coder).
- CTO communities: Rands Leadership Slack, LeadDev, CTO Craft, local CTO dinners.
- YC and other accelerator alumni lists (Saffron's own batch is not off limits).
- Companies who publicly adopted AI-allowed interviews (Canva, Shopify, Rippling, Coinbase, Red Hat) at the team level, not corporate.

## The offer (mirror of `13-company-pitch.md`)

**They get:** 60 days free, 10 assessments (15 for partners hiring more), two scenarios matched to their stack, every report hand-reviewed by us during the pilot, a weekly 30-minute call, first access to validity data, 20% off list for year one after conversion, input into the roadmap.

**They give:** at least 10 real candidates in 60 days; a 15-minute debrief after each hiring decision the report informed; permission to request a 6-item manager rating at 3 and 6 months for any hire who went through Dayone (with the hire's consent); a candidate experience survey link in their process; a logo and quote if satisfied; honest feedback including "we would not pay for this."

**Paperwork:** a one-page pilot agreement, a mutual NDA, a data processing agreement (we process candidate personal data on their behalf), and candidate notice language they must include in their invitation.

## Cadence

| When | What |
| --- | --- |
| Week 0 | Kickoff (60 min): their loop today, roles open, stack, what a bad hire cost them last time, what would make them switch. Configure the loop. Agree the candidate notice. |
| Weeks 1 to 8 | Weekly 30-min call: sessions run, what the report said, what they decided, what surprised them, what was missing. We hand-review every report before it reaches them in the first 4 weeks. |
| Each hiring decision | 15-min debrief within a week: did the report change or confirm the decision; which moments mattered; what would they have wanted. |
| Week 8 | Conversion conversation: usage recap, what they told us, pricing, plan. |
| Month 3 and 6 after each hire | Manager rating form (5 minutes). |

## What we measure

Per partner: sessions run, completion rate, time-to-report, report opened by how many people, debrief usage, decisions influenced, dispute count, candidate experience score, conversion.

Across partners: which scenarios get assigned (tests operational-vs-feature preference), which report sections get read (tests process-signal value), agent-vs-human agreement, price sensitivity at conversion.

## Concierge phase (before the product exists)

For the first 2 or 3 partners, run 3 to 5 real candidates each with a manual stack: a cloud dev environment with the scenario repo, a coding-agent CLI configured to log everything, a lightweight screen or terminal recording, and a hand-written report using the rubric. It will take a founder a full day per candidate. That is the point: we learn what the report needs to say before we build a pipeline to say it.

## Exit criteria

A partner is a success if they convert at list price minus the partner discount and keep assigning assessments in month 4. A partner is a learning if they do not convert and tell us precisely why. A partner is a failure if they run fewer than 5 candidates; drop them at week 4 and replace them.

## Risks

- Partners treat it as a free tool and never engage. Mitigation: the weekly call is mandatory; no call, no more assessments.
- Partners want customisation before value. Mitigation: library scenarios only in the pilot; forking is a paid-plan feature.
- We over-fit to one loud partner. Mitigation: weigh feedback by how many partners say it, and by whether it appears in the usage data.
